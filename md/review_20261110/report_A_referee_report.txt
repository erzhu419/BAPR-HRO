                     Referee Report on OPRE-2026-05-3065
   “Re-Ranking Stochastic Transit Hyperpaths Under Disruption:
            A Wasserstein-Robust Scoring Approach”


Summary
This paper studies stochastic public transit routing under real-time disruptions. Its central pro-
posal is to retain a precomputed stochastic hyperpath while dynamically re-ranking the available
connections using updated delay and cancellation information. The proposed Bayesian scoring
rule combines Normal-Gamma delay posteriors with Beta cancellation posteriors. Under an affine
arrival-cost model, the authors interpret the resulting pessimistic score through Wasserstein distri-
butionally robust optimization (DRO). The paper further presents theoretical guarantees, claims
machine-checked formal verification in Lean 4, and evaluates the algorithms on synthetic networks
and GTFS-Realtime data from Zurich.
   Despite the problem’s practical relevance, I have substantial reservations about the paper’s
mathematical validity, methodological novelty, and empirical conclusions. Several central theoret-
ical statements are false as written, while others rely on assumptions that are not established for
the implemented transit model. Moreover, the claimed Wasserstein equivalence holds under sub-
stantially narrower conditions than the presentation suggests and, under those conditions, reduces
to an established identity. These issues undermine the theoretical justification and positioning of
the contribution. I therefore recommend rejection.

     the difference in total journey cost. Yet arr(c) denotes destination arrival time, whereas the
     cost in Section 3.2 is the travel time to the next stop. The first equality in (18) therefore
     requires a valid decomposition showing why these per-stop destination-arrival gaps sum to
     total excess cost, including when the two policies visit different stops. The comparison policy
     must also be specified: if the optimal policy is not restricted to the same hyperpath, an
     excluded route may remain superior even with perfect estimates.
  5. The arguments do not establish the general claims about pessimism or the algorithm-specific
     regret guarantees. Theorem 6 assumes cost(k) = µk +ρσk , but the irreversibility of a boarding
     decision does not imply ρ > 0. An uncertain route can still be faster than a certain alternative;
     irreversibility alone does not justify a positive uncertainty penalty. Theorem 7 likewise treats
     a large regret upper bound as evidence of poor performance. A loose upper bound, however,
     does not establish large actual regret, which may be substantially smaller.

     Theorem 8 establishes an inequality for c/T + v/T , but it does not connect that expression
     to the actual per-journey regret of the Adaptive-β algorithm. This missing link prevents the
     inequality from serving as an algorithm-specific regret guarantee. Furthermore, if c and v are
     allowed to grow with T , the inequality alone does not establish the claimed O(1/T ) decay of
     squared regret; the dependence of these quantities on T must be controlled explicitly.
   Finally, the exposition falls below the standard expected for publication in OR. Numerous
passages read as formulaic rather than carefully reasoned, and the mathematical inconsistencies
documented above raise substantive concerns about the level of authorial validation. The paper
requires a thorough check of the definitions, assumptions, theorem statements, and proofs.

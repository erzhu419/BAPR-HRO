# V33 public-history reference and its scope

This is a reference implementation of standard belief-tree dynamic programming,
conditional Markov messages, and optimal stopping. No new algorithmic result is
claimed by naming these components or applying them to vehicle identities.

## Public history and the order of optimization

Let `h` contain only the successive chosen actions and publicly received
observations, and let `b(s|h)` be the exact finite-law posterior over private
states. All private states in this posterior share the same current action.
For action `a`, the transition atoms are first summed and grouped by the next
public observation `o`; the resulting posterior is `b'`. The reference uses

\[
 V(h,b)=\min_{a\in A(h)}\left[
 \sum_{\text{terminal }s,z}b(s)P(z\mid s,a)c(z)
 +\sum_{o\text{ nonterminal}}P(o\mid h,a)V(h\mathbin{\|}(a,o),b')\right].
\]

The minimum therefore follows integration over the currently hidden state.
Only a *subsequent observed* branch permits a different subsequent action.
Replacing this by a separate best action for each private world is a
clairvoyant relaxation. A finite sampled bank can also identify futures from
its finite set of prefixes; it is not accepted as an exact original-law input.

The generic solver accepts complete enumeration or original finite transition
laws. A history/atom/depth budget returns an interval. Unenumerated probability
keeps valid continuation bounds; it is neither discarded nor renormalized.
The supplied bounds must cover every continuation being bounded, including a
bad committed action. A feasible boarding value alone does not upper-bound
the cost after committing to WAIT. `unresolved_mass` refers to the completion
associated with the smallest upper bound, not the probability of all explored
branches across mutually exclusive policies.

## Natural shared-trip slices

`reference_selection.json` selects the first two qualifying V32 hubs before
reading any realized outcome. Root B has a retained direct continuation. Root A
has sequential retained alighting variants whose sole final vehicle is that
same B. B's direct option and all such A variants are kept. Other root vehicles
and other A variants are explicitly outside this diagnostic slice. Entry is
the hub's scheduled arrival, a declared local platform query; it is not an
assertion that the parent journey actually reached that history.

Both references retain the original V31 laws and 20-second grid. The generic
tree carries one latent root-B state throughout the journey. A's observations
are generated by the original service-killed conditional kernels. After
boarding A, no further absence evidence is collected at the root. An alighting
cost integrates B's root-to-tail conditional message and its service-killed
tail path, including MCT and missed departures. Service may resume before
boarding B; a failed segment after boarding is absorbed at the deadline.

These slices admit a simpler exact calculation. Partition the first public
event into A first, B first, a simultaneous offer, or no offer. Before readiness
an offer is observed but cannot be boarded. Given any such public history, B's
posterior is fixed after boarding A. For each public posterior `q`, first form
`G_r(t;q) = sum_b q(b) G_r(t;b)`, then apply ordinary chain stopping:

\[
 V_r(t;q)=\min\{G_r(t;q),\ H-\sum_{t'}K_r(t,t')[H-V_{r+1}(t';q)]\}.
\]

Averaging the separately optimized values for hidden B states would reveal B
before it was observed and is not this computation. The final root value
integrates the complete first-event partition. This exact decomposition relies
on two root offers, independent A/B laws, no external B observations after
leaving, fixed singleton-B final tails, and sufficient own-arrival states. It
does not solve the full V33 menu or establish a gain over its controls.

At the declared generic budget (500 histories, 1,000,000 atom terms), the two
trees remain unresolved: intervals are [31612.545, 32729.997] and
[30952.578, 32250.000] seconds. The standard structured references give
32377.680 and 32151.985 seconds, respectively; both lie inside those intervals.
Their first-event mass errors are below 1e-15. The expanded structured comparison
and decision receipts took about 0.62/0.42 seconds, with message compilation
charged separately; environment loading is additionally recorded in the run.
These are local reference costs, not a production-algorithm speed comparison.

### Same-information cheap controls

After the first root offer, at most one unobserved root vehicle remains. A legal
refusal is therefore exactly single-target waiting; simultaneous offers leave
no future root target. With common conditional A-chain continuation values,
conditional single-target and two-target controls reproduce the exact root
action at every public event. This is a structural equivalence for this slice,
not an empirical novelty claim.

The additional rolling-fixed and one-step-rollout A controls use the same B
posterior, all retained A stopping points, and the same tail values. Their
boarding values integrate their actual subsequent rolling actions. Every
root first-event action is then selected and integrated as an executable
policy. Against the exact reference, root Bellman regret and subsequent A
policy regret are separately weighted by their public-history occupancy.

| Same-information control | First slice expected loss from entry | Second slice expected loss from entry | Gap to exact |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Conditional single + standard chain stopping | 1297.680295 s | 1671.984763 s | 0 / 0 s |
| Conditional two-target + standard chain stopping | 1297.680295 s | 1671.984763 s | 0 / 0 s |
| Conditional single + rolling fixed-stop choice | 1297.680295 s | 1671.984763 s | 0 / 0 s |
| Conditional single + one-step rollout | 1297.680295 s | 1671.984763 s | 0 / 0 s |

Both slices have zero differing root actions, zero root disagreement probability,
zero expected onboard disagreement visits, and zero Bellman contributions for
all four controls. The two simpler onboard rules therefore also absorb the
reference on these original laws. No new algorithmic gain survives here. These
are model expectations on diagnostic slices, not observed journey improvements.

## Sufficient conditions for safe forgetting and message reuse

A public-history summary `S(h)` is sufficient if histories with the same summary
have the same legal actions and, for every legal action, the same conditional
joint law of the next public observation, next summary, and terminal cost.
Backward induction then gives identical values and an identical optimal
public policy. This controlled prediction condition is sufficient, not a
novel minimal-state theorem.

A vehicle/history component can consequently be discarded only when its
removal cannot change any future action, observable receipt, reward, or the
posterior of a retained component. Under independent vehicle laws, a trip
absent from every feasible continuation and future observation can be removed.
With common shocks or other coupling, graph absence alone does not prove this
condition. In the two-offer slices, earlier A arrivals may be forgotten after
its current observed arrival, while B's public posterior must remain.

Same-trip forward/backward messages can be reused between candidate occurrences
only when the underlying law and all observation emissions are identical.
Candidate-specific MCT, entry time, service survival and continuation objective
still apply. A new receipt/absence interval invalidates messages depending on
that trip's posterior; departing a platform ends that absence interval.
Shared trips are not independent copies, and correlated retained factors cannot
be separated merely because their candidate paths differ.

## Complexity and verification

For the generic tree, work is the sum, over reached public histories and legal
actions, of the enumerated transition atoms across the posterior's private
support. Both history count and joint support can grow exponentially. Ordinary
dependency-graph width alone does not bound this causal planning problem:
minimization cannot be moved before the observation that licenses it.

For `R` distinct public B posteriors, `B` root-B states and arrival support sizes
`n_r`, the specialized contractions cost
`O(R * (sum_r n_r*B + sum_r n_r*n_(r+1)))`, plus original message compilation
and the two-offer partition. This bound is for the stated structure only.
Nine focused tests cover public-prefix aliasing, observation-enabled actions,
truncation bounds, full probability, sampled-bank rejection, and equality of
the structured and fully enumerated references on two finite test problems.
Those test problems are unit tests, not fitted or empirical winning cases.

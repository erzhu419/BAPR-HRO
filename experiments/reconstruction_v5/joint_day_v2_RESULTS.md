# Consistent shared-day joint candidate

Shared-day rollout helps against a matching-marginal independent-offset control, but loses to the inexpensive Gaussian comparator once measured computation is charged. The candidate provides a coherent current-grid joint interface; it does not establish a practical advantage.

`JointDayBelief` mixes 13 ordered `EvolvingTripBelief` components with δ=-360,-300,…,360 s, Cauchy scale 43.5290348662 s from Oct1–2 and midpoint-bin prior masses with both tails assigned to endpoints. Every route mean shifts by δ/60; cancellation and transition increments are unchanged. On the current grids, $w_k\propto\pi_k\prod_{t\in H}Z_{kt}$ uses each revised trip's complete forward likelihood once. Event marginals, nominal clocks and private worlds use those weights; a world draws δ once for all trips. `IndependentDayMixtureBelief` has identical initial marginals but one persistent independent δ per trip, updated only by that trip's evidence.

The main protocol fixes Oct1–2 parameters, original Oct12/25/27 dates, OD0/1, four private samples, 512 transitions per decision, full physical menus and GTFS station transfer bounds. All dates were already examined before this candidate was designed. Corrected v2 has 24 baseline/shared rows, 12 matching-marginal control rows and a separately frozen 18-row charged comparison, all arrived. V1 is preserved separately. A reachable 12-minute-wait numerical issue was fixed by mixing raw operating marginal mass rather than subtracting rounded cancellation probability from one. V2 reproduces the same realized losses as v1 under the corrected source.

Thus shared rollout improves 314.83 s versus the matching-marginal control, but only 23.67 s versus the low-cost Gaussian comparator. Nominal shared inference worsens 126.50 s. The deterministic negative example is Oct27 OD0: after the same missed S12/no-vehicle wait, day-posterior mean becomes −116.13 s. Shared nominal predicts S3 already departed and takes S11; S3's measured departure is still catchable, and per-trip nominal arrives 759 s earlier. `joint_day_counterexample.py/json` preserves the common history and posthoc timeline, without treating one actual world as probability truth.

Charging measured query time leaves shared 210.67 s better than the matched control but 74.17 s worse than Gaussian. Mean charged-run end-to-end computation is 56.69/66.42/1.06 s for shared/control/Gaussian on this host. On Oct12 OD0 shared loss rises to 2846 versus Gaussian 1309; on Oct27 OD0 shared is 2239 versus Gaussian 3189. Exact per-case differences and the coupling-by-valuation contrast are in `joint_day_comparison.json`; all averages use six common cases. This readout ended the fixed experiment without additional dates or parameter selection.

Prior endpoint date-calibrated coverage is 99.33/92.65/96.31% at widths 601.4/602.4/601.3 s on Oct12/25/27; the candidate's separately computed Oct3–11 envelope is zero. Gaussian coverage is 98.37/85.39/91.46% at about 501 s. The Oct25 interval score improves 1046.2→999.9, but Oct12/27 scores worsen. This prior univariate improvement comes from the heavier marginal, which the per-trip control also has; it does not identify a benefit of sharing.

On the schedule-sorted every-twentieth transfer-pair subset (145 selected/date, with censoring retained), Brier is Gaussian .05501/.10592/.12544, matching-marginal per-trip .06345/.10716/.12239, shared .06039/.11565/.11042. Shared inference is worse than the matched control on Oct25 and better on Oct12/27; incoming-known raw interval coverage still misses 90% on Oct25/27. No universal probability-calibration improvement is established.

Limitations: this is a six-journey development mechanism experiment, not independent confirmation. Exact finite-mixture calculations are conditional on the current grids; off-grid observed clocks still change support and remain an adaptive approximation to a global continuous posterior. The finite δ endpoints discretize/clamp the Cauchy tails. A training-only 60-versus-30 s δ-grid diagnostic changes the three checked target means by at most .924 s and boarding probability by .00431; the 60 s grid was retained without development tuning. Original ordered-transition truncation bias is unchanged. Cross-trip posterior updating means the independent-trip relevance proof is not applied. Charged outcomes use measured host processing delays and may change under another workload; they are recorded separately from logical-time gains.

API: `event_belief=JointDayBelief(graph, minute=60, public_occurrences=False, **fit['increments'])` is accepted by the existing `CausalPlanningRouter`; no production router or original Evolving source was edited. The component posterior, observation, copy, event-distribution, mean and sample interfaces describe the same candidate. Nine tests pass via `python3 -m pytest tests/test_joint_day_belief.py tests/test_independent_day_mixture_belief.py -q`. Main receipts/protocols are `joint_day_v2_results*`, `joint_day_trip_control*`, `joint_day_coupling_charged*`; local results are `joint_day_local_v2*` and `joint_day_local_coupling_control.json`. Reproduce with `joint_day_run.py --workers 2`, `joint_day_control.py`, `joint_day_charged.py`, `joint_day_local.py`, then `joint_day_analyze.py`.
